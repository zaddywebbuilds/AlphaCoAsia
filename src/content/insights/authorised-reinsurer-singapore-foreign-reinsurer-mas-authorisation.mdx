An authorised reinsurer in Singapore is a foreign reinsurer that the Monetary Authority of Singapore has approved to accept reinsurance from Singapore-licensed insurers without establishing a licensed entity or branch in Singapore. If your group wants Singapore cedants as clients but does not need underwriters, claims staff or a regional hub on the island, authorisation is usually the faster and lighter route than a full reinsurer licence under the Insurance Act 1966. This article explains when a foreign reinsurer can write Singapore business without a local licence, what MAS looks at, what to assemble for the application, and how to plan the timeline.

## When a Foreign Reinsurer Can Write Singapore Business Without a Licence

Singapore regulates insurance business by the nature of the activity and the counterparty, not simply by the location of the reinsurer. A reinsurer that carries on reinsurance business in Singapore needs to be licensed as a reinsurer. A reinsurer that sits offshore and accepts cessions from Singapore cedants can instead seek authorisation, which gives the Singapore insurer a recognised counterparty for regulatory purposes.

The distinction matters in practice because Singapore insurers cannot treat every offshore reinsurer alike when they calculate their capital. Under the RBC2 framework, the credit a cedant takes for reinsurance depends on the quality of the counterparty, and an unrecognised reinsurer is treated less favourably than an authorised or licensed one. A foreign reinsurer without authorisation may still be able to write business, but it will be at a competitive disadvantage because the cedant carries a higher capital cost for the same cession. Authorisation is therefore as much a commercial decision as a legal one.

Three points to settle before deciding on the route:

- Whether you will place staff, underwriting authority or claims handling in Singapore. If yes, the activity is likely to be carried on in Singapore and the licensing analysis changes.
- Whether the business is reinsurance of Singapore risks, Singapore cedants only, or regional business that merely touches Singapore.
- Whether a licensed route would give you capacity that authorisation cannot, such as access to local talent and direct engagement with MAS supervisors.

## Reinsurer Licence vs Authorisation: What Actually Differs

The choice between a reinsurer licence and authorisation comes down to presence, capital and ongoing supervision. A licensed reinsurer is a Singapore-regulated entity. It must meet the Insurance Act requirements on paid-up capital, solvency under the RBC2 regime, appointed actuary and approved appointments, governance, risk management and ORSA. MAS supervises it directly and on an ongoing basis. For a detailed comparison of all the routes, see our guide to [Singapore insurer licence types](/insights/singapore-insurer-licence-types-direct-reinsurer-captive).

An authorised reinsurer is not licensed in Singapore and does not carry the same local obligations. MAS instead relies substantially on the strength of the reinsurer's home regulatory supervision, its financial standing and its willingness to meet the conditions of authorisation. The reinsurer does not build a Singapore balance sheet, and Singapore cedants simply see a counterparty that MAS has recognised.

The trade-offs are straightforward. Authorisation is quicker to establish, cheaper to maintain and easier to withdraw from. It does not, however, create a platform for regional underwriting, and it gives the reinsurer less visibility with the Singapore market than a local operation would. A reinsurer that expects Singapore to become a core underwriting centre usually outgrows the authorisation route and should model the licence economics early. ## What MAS Looks At in an Authorisation Application

MAS assesses authorisation applications against the Insurance Act and the subsidiary legislation and guidance that apply to authorised reinsurers. Applicants should check the current text of the relevant regulations and MAS application guidance on the MAS website before filing, because requirements and forms are updated from time to time.

In practice, expect MAS to focus on the following areas.

Home regulator and supervisory standing. MAS looks at whether the reinsurer is licensed and actively supervised by a recognised home regulator, and whether that regulator is willing to share supervisory information with MAS. A reinsurer domiciled in a jurisdiction with a thin supervisory framework faces more questions than one in a well-established regime.

Financial strength. This covers audited financial statements, solvency position under the home regime, and, where available, financial strength ratings from recognised agencies. Singapore cedants and MAS both pay close attention to the rating, because the rating also feeds into the cedant's counterparty credit risk charge under RBC2.

Business profile and reinsurance programme. MAS wants to understand the lines of business, the geographic spread, the retrocession programme and any concentration in Singapore business.

Governance and conduct. Information on directors, controllers and senior management, including fit and proper considerations, is required. See our article on [MAS fit and proper assessment](/insights/mas-fit-and-proper-assessment-insurers) for how MAS approaches the test.

Claims and dispute arrangements. MAS pays attention to how Singapore cedants will be paid, how claims disputes are resolved and whether the reinsurer will submit to Singapore jurisdiction or provide a local process agent. Enforceability of recoveries is a real prudential concern for the cedant.

## Documents and Information to Assemble

A complete first submission saves time. A practical checklist for a foreign reinsurer:

- Certificate of incorporation, constitution and group structure chart showing ultimate beneficial owners and controllers.
- Evidence of licence or authorisation from the home regulator, with confirmation of good standing.
- Audited financial statements for recent years and the latest regulatory solvency return from the home regime.
- Financial strength ratings and rating agency reports, where held.
- A description of the business plan for Singapore cedants, including lines of business, expected volumes and retrocession protection.
- Particulars of directors and senior management, with fit and proper declarations.
- A letter or undertaking from the home regulator on information sharing, where MAS requests it.
- Details of any appointed agent in Singapore for service of process and regulatory communications.
- Any further information that MAS requests during assessment.

A frequent cause of delay is a mismatch between the group structure chart and the information provided on controllers. Reconcile these before filing. Another is incomplete evidence on the home regulator's supervisory powers over the reinsurer, so request the relevant confirmation from the home regulator early.

## Application Route and Timeline Planning

The application is made to MAS through its licensing and authorisation channels, and MAS may engage with the applicant through clarification rounds. MAS does not commit to a fixed processing period for every application, because timing depends on the quality of the submission, the complexity of the group and how quickly the applicant answers questions. Treat any timeline as a planning assumption rather than a service commitment, and confirm the current MAS service standards before you set internal milestones.

A sensible planning sequence is:

1. Pre-filing analysis of the route, the home regulator position and the commercial case, covering the licence versus authorisation decision.
2. Preparation of the application and supporting documents, including any home regulator confirmations, which can take weeks to obtain.
3. Filing with MAS and responding to queries. Respond to clarification requests promptly and completely, because each loop adds to the total timeline.
4. Approval and conditions. Note any conditions and calendar the ongoing obligations before you begin accepting business.

Build slack into the plan if you intend to renew existing relationships with Singapore cedants on 1 January. Cedants will want to see authorisation in place before they renew treaty placements, since their capital treatment depends on it.

## Ongoing Obligations After Authorisation

Authorisation is not a one-off event. An authorised reinsurer is expected to keep MAS informed of material developments, including changes in control, significant changes in financial position, rating changes, regulatory action by the home regulator and material changes to the business plan. Cedants will also monitor these matters through their own reinsurance counterparty risk framework, and a downgrade can immediately affect how the cedant's capital charge is computed.

For Singapore cedants, the practical implication is that reinsurance credit risk governance applies to every counterparty, authorised or not. Insurers are expected to assess reinsurance risk within their ORSA, including the failure of a major reinsurer. Our guide on [reinsurance regulatory requirements across Asia Pacific](/insights/reinsurance-regulatory-requirements-apac) covers how this sits within wider counterparty and concentration management.

Reinsurers should also keep in mind that Singapore anti-money laundering and countering the financing of terrorism expectations are applied through cedants and through MAS supervision more broadly. Prepare for cedant due diligence requests on ownership, sanctions screening and source of funds, even where the reinsurer is not itself a Singapore licensee.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

Foreign reinsurers repeatedly stumble in the same places. They treat authorisation as a formality and underestimate the information MAS needs on the home regulator. They place staff in Singapore without checking whether that changes the licensing analysis. They file before the group structure and controller information is consistent across documents. They assume that authorisation delivers the same market standing as a licence, and then find that cedants want a local presence for large or long-tail treaties.

A related mistake is leaving the commercial case until last. Authorisation costs less than a licence, but it still requires management time, legal fees and a credible plan for the Singapore book. Test the economics against realistic premium volumes before committing.
