The group capital framework for a designated financial holding company (DFHC) with a licensed insurer subsidiary in Singapore is changing, and MAS has indicated that the revised requirements take effect from 1 January 2027. If your group is headed by a DFHC, the practical task before then is to rerun group solvency on the amended rules, identify which entities and instruments are affected, and take the results to the board and the group ORSA. This article summarises the proposals and the feedback outcome as reported publicly, and sets out how to prepare. It is not a substitute for the MAS notices and response papers; every parameter below should be confirmed against the final instruments.

## Where the framework sits

Capital for a licensed insurer in Singapore is set under the RBC2 framework, with Notice 133 covering valuation and capital. For groups, Notice FHC-N133 applies the same valuation and capital approach at the level of a DFHC that has a licensed insurer as a subsidiary. The group solvency position is expressed in the same way as at entity level: Group Financial Resources measured against a Group Total Risk Requirement.

That structure matters because changes to the group notice do not simply copy entity-level rules. Group capital has to deal with entities that are not insurers, with minority shareholders in subsidiaries, and with risks that sit outside any single licensed balance sheet. MAS issued consultation papers in July 2025 on these points, and on a standalone catastrophe risk requirement for general insurers, and later published its response to the group capital feedback.

Groups should also read this alongside our analysis of the [RBC2 capital framework](/insights/rbc2-capital-framework-singapore) and the [Notice 133 revisions](/insights/mas-notice-133-revisions-2026-cyclical-equity-charge-insurers), because the group notice draws on the entity-level calibration.

## The July 2025 proposals

MAS issued its consultation on enhancements to the group capital notice on 24 July 2025, with a closing date reported as 25 August 2025. A separate paper on a general insurance catastrophe risk requirement ran in parallel and was reported to close on 5 September 2025. The main group capital themes were as follows.

First, a risk charge for non-insurance entities. A group headed by a DFHC may own entities that are neither insurers nor banks, such as service companies, asset managers, technology subsidiaries or holding vehicles. The proposal introduces a risk charge for these non-insurance entities within the Group Total Risk Requirement, so that their risks are not left outside the group capital calculation. Public summaries also report that MAS may impose an additional capital charge where the prescribed formula does not capture all material risks of a particular entity.

Second, a limit on the recognition of capital from non-controlling interests (NCI) in Group Financial Resources. Where a subsidiary is not wholly owned, part of its capital belongs to minority holders and may not be freely available to absorb losses elsewhere in the group. MAS has indicated that it will retain the approach of limiting the amount of NCI recognised, and the question for each group is how large that limit bites on its structure.

Third, the catastrophe risk requirement. MAS proposed standardised scenarios for general insurance catastrophe risk, covering natural and man-made events. As reported, flooding was proposed as the prescribed natural catastrophe scenario for Singapore, man-made scenarios were adapted from the Insurance Capital Standard, and insurers would also run an annual own bespoke scenario. We have not confirmed the final calibration, and groups with general insurance subsidiaries should read the response paper for the scenario definitions and the way the result enters the Total Risk Requirement.

## The timeline

MAS published its response to the group capital consultation in September 2026, and the changes for DFHCs (Licensed Insurer) are reported to take effect from 1 January 2027. That leaves roughly six weeks from the date of this article to the effective date, which is short for a group that has not yet run the numbers.

The working timetable for most groups is therefore compressed:

1. Confirm the effective dates and transitional provisions in the final notice amendments, entity by entity.
2. Complete a dry-run calculation of Group Financial Resources and Group Total Risk Requirement on the new basis at the latest quarter-end.
3. Agree the management response, including any change to the group solvency target, with the board risk committee before the first reporting date after 1 January 2027.
4. Update the group ORSA projections and documentation so that the next assessment reflects the new rules.

## What changes for the group structure

The non-insurance entity charge and the NCI limit are structural. Their effect depends on what the group owns and how it owns it, which means the first piece of work is an entity map, not a model run.

Start with a register of every entity in the DFHC perimeter, with its legal form, ownership percentage, regulatory status, and the nature of its business. Identify which entities are licensed insurers, which are regulated non-insurers (for example fund managers or financial advisers), and which are unregulated. The risk charge will fall hardest where a group holds large unregulated or lightly regulated operating businesses, because those previously did not attract a group-level charge calibrated to their own risk.

Then look at minority holdings. For each subsidiary with outside shareholders, record the NCI share of capital and any contractual restrictions on moving capital, such as dividend limits, shareholder agreements or joint venture reserved matters. If MAS limits the recognition of NCI capital, a group with material joint ventures or partly owned insurers may see Group Financial Resources fall even though no economic loss has occurred.

Intragroup arrangements also need review. Loans, guarantees, reinsurance cessions and shared service agreements between the licensed insurer and other group entities can change the picture under a group view. A cession to an affiliate that looks efficient at entity level may offer less relief once the group perimeter is considered.

## Governance and the group ORSA

A change to the group capital rules is a governance matter as well as a calculation matter. Under Notice FHC-N126, a financial holding company is expected to run a group ERM framework and a group ORSA covering the consolidated risk profile; our guide to [MAS Notice 126](/insights/mas-notice-126-enterprise-risk-management-insurers-guide) explains the group extension. The group ORSA is where the board should see the effect of the new capital rules.

We suggest that the group ORSA for the first cycle after the effective date does the following:

- Projects the group solvency position over the planning horizon on the new rules, with the non-insurance entity charge and NCI limit modelled explicitly.
- Tests the effect of a severe catastrophe event on the general insurance subsidiaries, using the prescribed scenarios and the group's own bespoke scenario, where they apply.
- Shows the management actions available to restore the position, including capital injections, restructuring of minority holdings and changes to intragroup reinsurance, and states how long each would take.
- Records which assumptions are judgement calls, such as the treatment of entities where MAS may apply an additional charge.

MAS has previously commented on weaknesses in group ORSA work, including that group risks are not always assessed on a consolidated basis; see our summary of [MAS findings on group ORSA](/insights/orsa-group-risk-assessment-singapore-insurers-mas-findings). A new capital charge on non-insurance entities makes that weakness more visible, because the entities in question were often the ones least covered by the existing risk assessment.

## Practical steps before 1 January 2027

For a DFHC group, the following sequence is realistic in the time available.

1. Obtain the final notice text and the MAS response, and prepare a clause-level summary of changes for the board and the audit committee.
2. Build the entity and ownership register described above and reconcile it to the legal entity chart filed with MAS.
3. Calculate the new charge for each non-insurance entity and the NCI recognition limit, and bridge the result from the position on the old rules.
4. Review the catastrophe exposure of the general insurance subsidiaries against the new standardised scenarios and the bespoke scenario process, and confirm who owns the annual bespoke scenario.
5. Update the capital policy, dividend policy and group solvency target, and brief the MAS relationship team where the change in position is material.
6. Document the work, including the judgements made, so that it can be shown to the supervisor on request.

Two cautions apply. First, a dry run on provisional parameters is useful but should be labelled as such, and the numbers should be refreshed when the final instrument is read line by line. Second, where an entity falls into a grey area, such as a captive, a protected cell arrangement or a special purpose vehicle, seek early clarification from MAS and record the outcome.
