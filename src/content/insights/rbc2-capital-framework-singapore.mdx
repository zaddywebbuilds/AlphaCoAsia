## Background: Why RBC2 Was Introduced

Singapore's Risk-Based Capital framework has been the foundation of the local insurance solvency regime since 2004. The original framework, now referred to as RBC1, served the market well through a period of significant growth and development but was designed for a different era. Product structures have become more complex, the investment environment has shifted materially and the global standard-setting work of the International Association of Insurance Supervisors has raised the bar for what a credible solvency framework should look like.

RBC2, which MAS developed through an extended consultation process involving multiple quantitative impact studies, is the result. It replaces the more mechanical approach of RBC1 with a framework that is more sensitive to the actual risks insurers run, more aligned with international standards and better at distinguishing between insurers with genuinely different risk profiles.

For insurers operating in Singapore, the transition to RBC2 has capital consequences that vary significantly by business model, asset mix and product portfolio. This briefing sets out the key technical changes and what they mean in practice.

## Key Changes to Insurance Risk Treatment

The treatment of insurance risk under RBC2 is more granular than its predecessor. Under RBC1, insurance risk charges were applied at a relatively high level, with limited differentiation between products or risk profiles within a category. RBC2 introduces more specific treatment for:

**Life insurance risk.** The framework now distinguishes more precisely between the risk drivers relevant to different product types: mortality risk, longevity risk, morbidity risk and lapse risk are treated as distinct exposures with separate calibration. This matters because an insurer writing primarily protection products faces a different mortality and morbidity profile from one predominantly writing investment-linked products.

**General insurance risk.** Premium risk and reserve risk are treated separately, with premium risk reflecting the uncertainty in estimating future claims from premiums written and reserve risk reflecting the uncertainty in the adequacy of existing reserves. The calibration of these charges depends on the lines of business written, with property catastrophe exposure treated distinctly from liability or health lines.

**Catastrophe risk.** RBC2 introduces an explicit catastrophe risk charge that was not present in the same form under RBC1. For general insurers with material property exposure in Singapore and the surrounding region, this is a potentially significant addition to the capital requirement. The catastrophe scenarios used in calibration reflect the regional natural hazard environment.

## Market Risk and Credit Risk Treatment

The treatment of market risk and credit risk has been overhauled under RBC2 in ways that affect most insurers regardless of their product profile.

**Interest rate risk** is now captured through scenario-based testing rather than a simple factor approach. The interest rate scenarios applied reflect both parallel shifts and changes in the shape of the yield curve. For life insurers with long-duration liabilities, particularly guaranteed savings products and annuity portfolios, the interaction between interest rate risk and the duration of liabilities is now captured more precisely.

**Equity and property risk** charges are calibrated at a level that reflects historical loss experience in significant market downturns. Insurers with material listed equity holdings or direct property exposure should expect higher capital charges than under RBC1, particularly if their asset allocation has not been reviewed in light of the new framework.

**Credit risk** is now applied on a counterparty-by-counterparty basis for significant exposures rather than at a portfolio level. This affects reinsurance recoverables, bond portfolios and bank deposits. The credit quality of reinsurers used by general insurers becomes a more direct driver of the capital requirement under RBC2.

**Currency risk** is treated with more granularity under RBC2, recognising that currency mismatches between assets and liabilities create a different risk profile from matched positions.

## Changes to Capital Quality Requirements

RBC2 introduces stricter requirements on the quality of capital that can be counted toward the solvency requirement. The framework establishes a tiering system for capital, with Tier 1 capital being the most loss-absorbing and Tier 3 being the least.

**Tier 1 capital** consists primarily of ordinary paid-up share capital and retained earnings for locally incorporated insurers, or the assigned capital and retained surplus for branches. The key requirement is that Tier 1 capital is genuinely available to absorb losses and is not subject to encumbrances or conditions that reduce its quality.

**Tier 2 capital** can include certain hybrid instruments and subordinated debt that meet specified criteria on permanence, loss absorbency and subordination. The criteria are more rigorous than under RBC1, and some instruments that previously qualified may not meet the RBC2 standards.

**Tier 3 capital** has limited recognition and is subject to strict limits on the proportion of total capital requirements it can satisfy.

The practical implication is that insurers with complex capital structures, including those that have issued hybrid instruments or have group support arrangements, need to reassess the eligibility of their capital components under the new framework.

## Differing Impacts on Life and General Insurers

The impact of RBC2 is not uniform. The changes in insurance risk treatment, market risk sensitivity and capital quality requirements interact differently depending on the business model.

**Life insurers** are most affected by the changes to interest rate risk treatment and the more granular approach to mortality, longevity and lapse risk. Insurers with significant guaranteed savings portfolios, particularly those written during the low-rate era with guaranteed crediting rates, face a more demanding assessment under RBC2 than under RBC1 in a rising rate environment.

Participating business, which remains significant in Singapore's life market, is treated with more precision under RBC2 in terms of how the policyholder and shareholder portions of risk are separated for capital purposes.

**General insurers** are most affected by the introduction of the catastrophe risk charge and the changes to market risk treatment for investment portfolios. General insurers that have historically held significant equity or property allocations may find their capital position more sensitive to market movements under RBC2.

Reinsurance credit quality is a more direct driver of the capital position for general insurers under RBC2, which has prompted some insurers to review their reinsurance panel composition.

## Practical Steps for Assessing Your Position

Understanding your position under RBC2 requires quantitative analysis specific to your balance sheet, not just an understanding of the framework in the abstract.

**Step 1: Run the numbers under the new framework.** The starting point is a quantitative impact assessment that applies the RBC2 charges to your current balance sheet and liability profile. This will reveal where your capital position has changed from RBC1 and by how much.

**Step 2: Understand the drivers.** Once you have the aggregate impact, decompose it into the risk components that are driving the change. This enables targeted analysis of where capital efficiency can be improved and informs any capital management actions.

**Step 3: Review your capital management plan.** If the RBC2 assessment reveals a materially different capital position from RBC1, your capital management plan should be updated to reflect the new framework. This includes any trigger levels for management actions, dividend policy and internal capital targets above the regulatory minimum.

**Step 4: Assess your investment strategy.** The changed sensitivity to market risk under RBC2 may warrant a review of your strategic asset allocation. The capital cost of different asset classes changes under the new framework, and an investment strategy that was appropriate under RBC1 may not remain optimal.

**Step 5: Review reinsurance arrangements.** For general insurers, the credit quality of reinsurers has become a more direct driver of the capital position. Reviewing your reinsurance panel against the new credit risk treatment is a worthwhile exercise.

**Step 6: Engage the board.** RBC2 changes the numbers the board needs to understand and monitor. The capital ratios, sensitivities and stress scenarios presented to the board should be updated to reflect the new framework, and board members should understand the key drivers of change.

## Briefing Your Board on RBC2

One of the more practically challenging aspects of the transition to RBC2 is communicating the changes to board members who may have become familiar with the RBC1 framework over many years.

The most effective board briefings on RBC2 do three things. They explain what changed and why the changes were made, without requiring board members to understand every technical detail. They show the specific impact on the insurer's capital position, decomposed into the key drivers. And they set out what management is doing in response, including any investment, reinsurance or capital management actions being considered.
