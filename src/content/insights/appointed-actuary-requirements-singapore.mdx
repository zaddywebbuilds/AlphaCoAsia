## What Is an Appointed Actuary?

The Appointed Actuary (AA) is a regulatory role prescribed under Singapore's Insurance Act. Licensed insurers that transact long-term insurance business (essentially life insurers and composite insurers) are required to appoint an actuary who meets specific qualifications and experience criteria set by MAS. The Appointed Actuary holds statutory responsibilities that cannot be delegated -- they belong personally to the individual appointed to the role.

The Appointed Actuary is not just a technical resource. They are a regulatory gatekeeper: someone MAS can look to for an independent actuarial assessment of the insurer's liabilities, solvency and financial condition. MAS expects the Appointed Actuary to act with professional independence and to report concerns to the board and, where required, directly to the regulator.

## Who Must Appoint an Appointed Actuary?

Under the Insurance Act, every insurer licensed to carry on long-term insurance business in Singapore must appoint an Appointed Actuary. This includes:

- Life insurers
- Composite insurers (writing both life and general business)

General insurers are not required to appoint an Appointed Actuary under the Insurance Act, though they may choose to engage actuarial support for reserving, pricing and capital work. Certain general insurance functions -- such as the actuarial certification required under the RBC2 framework -- may be performed by a signing actuary who is not an Appointed Actuary.

Branch operations of foreign insurers operating in Singapore are also required to appoint an Appointed Actuary for their Singapore long-term business.

## Qualification Requirements

MAS prescribes minimum qualifications for the Appointed Actuary role. The individual must:

- Be a Fellow of a recognised actuarial body (the Society of Actuaries, the Institute and Faculty of Actuaries, and the Singapore Actuarial Society are among those recognised)
- Have relevant experience in life insurance actuarial work
- Meet any additional fit and proper requirements that MAS may apply

MAS must be notified when an Appointed Actuary is appointed or when an appointment changes. In practice, MAS expects insurers to notify them before the appointment takes effect, and to seek confirmation that MAS has no objection to the proposed appointee.

## Statutory Responsibilities

The Appointed Actuary's responsibilities are set out in the Insurance Act and MAS Notices. They include:

**Actuarial valuation of long-term insurance liabilities.** The Appointed Actuary is responsible for the annual valuation of the insurer's long-term insurance liabilities and for certifying that the liabilities have been computed in accordance with applicable actuarial standards and regulatory requirements.

**Actuarial certificate on the Financial Condition Report.** Under MAS requirements, the Appointed Actuary must prepare or certify the actuarial elements of the insurer's Financial Condition Report, including the risk-based capital assessment.

**ORSA input.** The Appointed Actuary plays a central role in the Own Risk and Solvency Assessment, providing actuarial input to the forward-looking capital projections and stress scenarios.

**Reporting adverse findings.** If the Appointed Actuary forms the view that the insurer is in a financially unsound condition, or that its financial condition is deteriorating in a way that may render it unsound, they are required to report this to the board and, if the board does not act, directly to MAS. This duty to report overrides any confidentiality obligation to the insurer.

**Board access.** The Appointed Actuary must have direct access to the board and must be able to attend board meetings and present their findings independently of management.

## Independence Requirements

MAS places significant weight on the independence of the Appointed Actuary. The actuary should be able to form and express professional views without interference from management, and should have the authority and access needed to do their job effectively.

For external Appointed Actuaries, independence is relatively clear-cut. For internal Appointed Actuaries (employees of the insurer), MAS expects governance arrangements that protect the actuary's ability to report independently. This typically means:

- A reporting line that does not create conflicts of interest
- Direct board access that does not pass through management
- Clear documentation of the actuary's mandate and authority

## Internal vs External Appointed Actuary

Insurers may appoint either an internal actuary (an employee) or an external actuary (from an actuarial firm or advisory practice) as their Appointed Actuary. The choice involves trade-offs.

**Internal Appointed Actuary.** An internal AA has deep knowledge of the business and may be more embedded in the actuarial production process. However, managing independence for an internal AA requires careful governance, and the insurer carries the risk if the individual leaves.

**External Appointed Actuary.** An external AA brings independence that is structurally easier to demonstrate, and provides access to a broader firm resource base. However, the external AA needs to invest time in understanding the business in sufficient depth to fulfil their statutory responsibilities credibly.

Many smaller Singapore life insurers use external Appointed Actuaries, typically from actuarial consultancies with Singapore insurance expertise.

## What Happens When the Appointed Actuary Changes

When an Appointed Actuary leaves or is replaced, the insurer must notify MAS promptly and ensure there is no period during which the role is unfilled. Gaps in the Appointed Actuary appointment are a compliance breach.

The incoming Appointed Actuary is expected to review prior actuarial work and satisfy themselves as to its quality before providing certifications based on it. Thorough handover documentation is essential.

## Common Issues in Appointed Actuary Arrangements

**Scope conflicts.** Where the Appointed Actuary also performs consulting work for the same insurer, conflicts of interest can arise. The consulting work should not compromise the AA's independence in their statutory role.

**Board access in practice.** Board access that exists formally but is rarely exercised does not provide genuine independence. MAS expects the Appointed Actuary to attend relevant board discussions, not merely to receive the minutes.

**Qualification verification.** Insurers are responsible for verifying that the Appointed Actuary's qualifications remain current and that any continuing professional development requirements are met.
